    How does this work?

    Every weekday on this page comes from the Doomsday algorithm (also called the Doomsday rule), which mathematician John Conway designed to be done in your head (here he is explaining it). It rests on one fact: in any year, 4/4, 6/6, 8/8, 10/10 and 12/12 all fall on the same weekday, which Conway called the year's doomsday. Every month has a date like that.

    To find a weekday, you work out the year's doomsday from a weekday for the century and a small number for the year. Then you count from the nearest doomsday date in your month. The working above shows each step for the date you entered.

    Why 1583?

    The Gregorian calendar began in October 1582. Before that, and in some countries for much longer (Britain and its colonies until September 1752, Russia until 1918; see when each country switched), dates were kept in the Julian calendar, which needs a different anchor. The Doomsday app teaches the Julian version too.
