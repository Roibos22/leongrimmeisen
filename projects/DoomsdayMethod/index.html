    How it works

    The Doomsday algorithm in four steps

    Steps 1 and 2 give you the year's doomsday. Steps 3 and 4 turn it into an answer. For a date this year, you can skip straight to step 3.

    Century anchor

    One weekday per century. Four of them, and they repeat every 400 years.

    1900s → Wednesday2000s → Tuesday

    Year offset

    A number from 0 to 6 from the last two digits of the year. Add it to the anchor.

    y + y ÷ 4take away sevens

    Month doomsday

    One easy date in every month that always lands on the year's doomsday.

    4/4 · 6/6 · 8/89-to-5 at the 7-ElevenPi Day

    Count

    Step to your date in sevens, which are free, then a few days either way.

    ±7 changes nothingnever more than 3

    Questions

    Frequently asked

    Who invented the Doomsday method?

    The mathematician John Horton Conway devised it in 1973, building on a method Lewis Carroll published in 1887. Conway named each year's shared weekday its doomsday. You can watch Conway explain it himself.

    How long does it take to learn?

    Dates in the current year take an afternoon, because you only need the twelve month dates and a short count. Any date in the 1900s and 2000s takes a couple of weeks of a few minutes a day. Speed comes with practice after that.

    Do I need a good memory?

    No. The whole method is about a dozen facts: a weekday for each century and one easy date per month, most of which pair up. The arithmetic never goes beyond adding small numbers and taking away sevens.

    Does it work for any year?

    It works for any date in the Gregorian calendar, past or future, because the century anchors repeat every 400 years. For dates before a country switched from the Julian calendar, such as Britain before September 1752, you need a small Julian adjustment, which the app teaches.

    Is the Doomsday app free?

    It is free to start: the first 16 lessons, three drills, 30 Classic dates a day and the Daily Challenge every day. Doomsday Pro is a one-time purchase that unlocks the full course, every drill and Time Attack. There is no subscription.
